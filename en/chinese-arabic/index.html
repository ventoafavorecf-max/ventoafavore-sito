Why it matters

A language is not for talking. It is for being re-read.

At a trade fair in the Gulf, everyone speaks English: it is the language appointments are made in, and the first round of questions. The point comes later.

The sheet left on the table is re-read in an office, days afterwards, by someone who was not at the fair. Often an engineer, who reads English more slowly than the person who collected it. At that moment, two identical sheets are no longer identical: one is read in the language of the house, the other is not.

It holds for the Gulf and it holds for China. And it holds the opposite way round from how people usually think: the language is not for the negotiation, it is for the moment when none of your people are there to explain.

The limits

Two things we do not do, and it is better said first

No regulated documents, in any language: instruction manuals, food labels, declarations of conformity, safety data sheets. The language is set by the rules and the responsibility stays with the manufacturer. In Chinese and Arabic that rule counts double.

No hosting of a site inside China. A site hosted outside China opens slowly there, and being properly present requires a filing with the Chinese authorities that we do not have. We deliver the file, finished: the hosting is chosen by someone who knows that ground.
