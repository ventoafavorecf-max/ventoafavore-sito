The problem, in practice

The difference shows in a single sentence

Under ‘Translated’, the literal Italian version: grammatically correct, and skipped over by the reader. Under ‘Rewritten’, the same information in the order an Italian buyer looks for it. The three companies are invented; the mistakes are typical.

Cosmetics maker · product page‘Premium bio-cellulose masks with a skin-adaptive, ultra-fine matrix structure.’
Translated

Maschere premium in bio-cellulosa con una struttura a matrice ultra-fine adattiva alla pelle.

Rewritten

Maschera in biocellulosa: la trama aderisce al viso senza staccarsi, anche sul contorno occhi. Posa 15 minuti, monouso, confezione da 5.

The first line is correct Italian and tells a beautician nothing she can use. The second says what she needs in order to resell it: it stays in place around the eyes, fifteen minutes, single use, box of five.

Machinery manufacturer · button on the product page‘Enquire now’
Translated

Indagare ora

Rewritten

Richiedi un’offerta

‘Indagare’ is what the police do: the software read ‘enquire’ as ‘investigate’. It sits on the one button that turns a visitor into a contact. ‘Richiedi un’offerta’, ask for a quotation, is what Italian manufacturers write there.

Industrial supplier · contact page‘Monday – Thursday, 8:00 – 17:00’
Translated

Lunedi – Giovedi, 8:00 – 17:00

Rewritten

Da lunedì a giovedì, 8:00 – 17:00. Assistenza in italiano: chiedete del referente per l’Italia.

Two missing accents tell every Italian reader that no Italian has read the page. Nobody writes in to point it out. The rewritten line also answers an industrial buyer’s first question: who picks up the phone, and in which language.

A fact can be checked, and so it can be believed.
