The problem, in practice

The difference shows in a single sentence

Under ‘Translated’, the literal translation from the Italian: correct, and skipped over by the reader. Under ‘Rewritten’, the same information in the order a buyer looks for it. The companies are invented; the mistakes are typical.

Balsamic vinegar maker · product page‘Un balsamico frutto di una passione tramandata da generazioni.’
Translated

A balsamic vinegar fruit of a passion handed down from generations.

Rewritten

Aged a minimum of three years in oak, chestnut and cherry. Two ingredients, no thickeners, no caramel. Same cellar since 1931.

The same claim. The second version can be checked, and so it can be believed.

Winery · wine sheet‘Un Lambrusco secco, dal perlage fine e persistente.’
Translated

A dry Lambrusco, with a fine and persistent perlage.

Rewritten

Dry – not the sweet Lambrusco sold abroad in the 1980s. Bottle-fermented, pale rosé, high acidity. Serve chilled, with cured meats.

Abroad, the name ‘Lambrusco’ calls up the wrong idea. The text has to clear that away first.

Machinery maker · request from a foreign distributor‘Centralina oleodinamica compatta, ideale per ogni esigenza applicativa.’
Translated

Compact hydraulic power unit, ideal for every application need.

Rewritten

3 kW, 250 bar, 11.8 l/min at 160 bar. 20-litre tank, IP55. Run-tested at 250 bar; test sheet with every unit.

A purchasing office does not buy from a description. It buys from a line it can paste into its own specification.
